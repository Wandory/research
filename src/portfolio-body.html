The five studies

Click any study to open it: findings, the tables behind them, the parts that are not obvious from the summary, and every file it produced.

What it found

  • Four protocols out of eight pay out more than they earn. Morpho handed out $30.6M with zero revenue, Fluid $21.8M against $12.9M, Euler $6.6M against $4.7M.
  • The split between real and printed yield runs from 1.00 to 0.45. SparkLend never paid a single day of rewards; Euler v2 prints more than half of what it shows.
  • Rewards buy nothing in lending. A difference in differences study on 91 reward switches: turning rewards on produced zero excess TVL growth against the protocol's own trend.
  • The headline rate decays with the horizon. Over 30 days the advertised APY is honest everywhere; over 365 days the median depositor got 0.64 of it at Moonwell.

What the subsidy cost

Dependency is subsidies divided by protocol revenue. Net margin is revenue minus subsidies over average supply, annualised.

Not obvious from the summary

  • One inclusion rule decides the whole result. Counting every pool day gives Compound v3 a 9.4% coverage figure, because collateral pools pay no interest at all. The rule that fixes it turned out to be written in the protocol's own documentation.
  • The first version of this study was wrong, and the report says so. It mixed parent and child protocol slugs, which moves Spark's fees by 49%, and it mixed two revenue definitions, which made Venus look like a 0.84% take rate instead of 17.8%.
  • Euler v2 gets no conclusion. Its rank moves six places out of eight depending on the weighting, so the report states that no conclusion is available rather than picking the flattering angle.

What it found

  • The rate is commoditised. Median 3.40% a year across the thirty largest vaults, with a typical band of 1.08 points. Choosing a curator barely moves what you earn.
  • The fee does not track the result. Curators charge from 0% to 25% of the yield for rates that sit within one point of each other.
  • Risk is where they differ. Some vaults sit entirely in blue chip collateral, others hold none of it at the same rate.
  • Depositors move in herds. Sixteen of the twenty six vaults with a full year of history lost more than 30% of their assets, while one grew from $13M to $419M.

Not obvious from the summary

  • Six vaults holding $473M report impossible rates, up to 297,995% a year, with no listed curator. Two of them are named Not Gauntlet and Duplicated Key. The vault namespace is not moderated, and the interface shows a name and a number.
  • The data comes from Morpho's own API, not from an aggregator, so the protocol itself splits the rate into total and excluding rewards. That makes the reward share a protocol fact rather than an estimate.
  • Spikes exist but cannot be caught. Thirty vaults produced 238 days with a rate above 20% a year, and they last hours, at the moment utilisation hits the ceiling.

What it found

  • Two models on one market. Uniswap v3 and v4 hold liquidity with no emissions at all. Aerodrome v1 prints nine tenths of what its LPs earn.
  • Here the money works. Median excess TVL 90 days after rewards are switched on is +39.5%, against zero in lending.
  • And it leaves. Switching rewards off costs Aerodrome a median 10.2% of pool TVL against its own venue trend, negative in 70% of cases.
  • Exchanges spend more than lenders, over a shorter window. $96.7M across 143 pools against $73M across 478 lending pools over a longer period.

What happens around a reward switch

Excess is the change in pool TVL minus the change of the whole venue over the same days. The last two rows are the lending result, for comparison.

Not obvious from the summary

  • Curve moves the other way and the data explains why. After a switch off its pools grew faster than the venue, because a Curve reward rarely disappears, it falls from 2.3% to 0.4% and the pool stays inside the emission vote.
  • The advertised yield here excludes impermanent loss. The study measures the composition of the shown rate, not what the liquidity provider ended up with, and says so in the limitations rather than in a footnote.
  • Emission is counter cyclical. At Aerodrome the fee share of yield fell from 0.76 to 0.31 in five months while the reward rate climbed to 104% a year. They pay more exactly when trading pays less.

What it found

  • Honest for a month, not for a year. Over 30 days every issuer delivers what it shows. Over 365 days the median holder got 0.81 of it at Ethena and 0.88 at Sky.
  • The mechanism is visible in the shape of the curve. Treasuries draw a flat line, a funding rate draws the market cycle, a governance decision draws steps.
  • Money follows the rate down. Ethena went from $5.9B to $1.35B as its rate fell from above 7% to 4%.
  • A high opening rate is an invitation, not a level. Midas opened at 10.4% and sits at 5.0%, Falcon at 7.9% and sits at 3.7%.

Not obvious from the summary

  • Predictability is the product. Ondo pays the least of the eight and misses its own shown rate in only 9% of monthly windows. Everyone else misses in 30 to 45%.
  • One issuer prints most of its yield. Usual USD0 showed 14.7% of its own rate against 97.6% in token rewards at the peak, so 87% of the number on the screen was printed.
  • The same decay appears in lending. Two different markets, one method, and the same answer: the shown rate describes a month and not a year.

What it covers

  • The traded unit. What is actually being sold once execution is unbundled from settlement, and what a buyer can enforce.
  • Pricing. The unit priced under several models and compared against existing markets for compute.
  • Failure modes. Verification cost, latency, MEV capture and operator concentration, each with the condition that breaks the market.

Written as a contribution to the protocol rather than as marketing, which is why it spends as much space on what would go wrong as on the design itself.

How the work is done

The same three rules in every study, which is why they can be read together.

Collection

Protocol APIs first, aggregators second. Daily series pulled pool by pool with retry and backoff, a documented failure rate, and every intermediate table saved so the result can be rebuilt without touching the network again.

Verification

Nothing is published on one calculation. Weightings swapped, medians against means, small and young units excluded, years split, and in the lending study the whole panel collected a second time a day later to see whether the answer moved.

Honesty

Missing stays missing, never zero. Limitations sit in the same document as the findings, before the conclusions. When a result of mine broke a rule of the project, it came out of the report, and the report says which result and why.
